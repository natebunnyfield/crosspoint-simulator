## 1. The four readings, and the rule

The spacing doc records three measures that were each believed and each wrong,
so no pair moved on one reading alone. Each pair in the three words, in both
styles, was read four ways. All values are in font units (1000 upm).
**+ means looser than it should be.**

- **White:** `rsb(L) + kern + lsb(R)`, with the kern taken from the GPOS by
  HarfBuzz. This is the honest white (`gap_measure.py`, round 388's
  instrument).
- **r, his bench residual:** the white now minus his target. His target is the
  pair's white in the 09-20 bench fonts plus his delta. Where the pair was
  re-asked, the delta is the mean of his two answers.
  Instrument: `instruments/r389_bench_residuals.py`.
- **d4, Measure 4 as a pair interaction:** `cmp_space_2d`'s 2-D closest
  approach, as `g(ab) − med_x g(ax) − med_x g(xb) + med_xy g(xy)`, with x
  over `noeuimhac`. This is what the pair does beyond what its two letters'
  own sides already do. The same number is taken in each reference face of
  the style's set (`refsets.py`), and d4 is Albo's value minus the
  references' median. It subtracts the face's tracking and both letters'
  bearings, so only the pair is left.
  Instrument: `instruments/r389_pair_interaction.py`.
- **d5:** the same interaction on Measure 5 (`cmp_word_white`), at a clamp of
  1.5 counters. At the default 1.0 the roman saturates: every pair in the
  three words read 134–137 units, which is the clamp itself.

**The rule.** A pair moves if both of these hold:

- r ≥ 12, which is past his 10.8 repeatability, and
- d4 or d5 agrees with r in sign.

A pair also moves if r ≥ 20 on its own. That is twice his repeatability, and
the re-ask showed that on large-residual rows he repeats himself (14.0) while
the model misses (31.9) (`albo-kerning-noise-floor-2026-09-25.md`, Finding 2).
Each value is round 388's arithmetic: the target white minus the white at round
388, added to what the pair carries (`_shipped + d`). The bold cuts inherit the
same change.

## 3. The instrument mistake, and the correction

The first commit, `971723f`, moved roman `rt` +14 and `st` −14, and italic `te`
+18 and `es` +22. It read d4 and d5 from two backgrounded runs, one per style,
whose output shared one terminal. They printed in **completion order**. The
italic has seven references to the roman's eight, so it finished first, and
its table was read as the roman's.

Re-run one style per process, to a file:

- `rt` roman flipped from "all three agree tight" to r −14 against d4 +25.
- `st` flipped from +14/+21 to +14/0.
- `te` italic flipped from −18/−14 to −18/+8.

All three were withdrawn in `49deaff`. Italic `es` survives on the ≥20 arm.
Roman `rd` joins it on the same arm.

The per-file run (`instruments/r389_pair_evidence.py`, with n- and o-neutral
interactions) was never affected. Neither was `bench_outliers.py evidence`,
which is single-process. Its roman `tu` reads d4 +55.7 against the corrected
table's +56, and that agreement is how the swap was caught.
`r389_pair_interaction.py` now carries a header warning against running two
styles into one terminal.

## 5. The outlier bench

**Page:** `tools/wedge_serif/bench/outliers-2026-09-25.html`. It is
self-contained, with both round-389 fonts embedded
(`bench/fonts-2026-09-25/`, the `49deaff` build). It is the re-ask page's
template, taken from `bench_reask.PAGE_TEMPLATE` rather than copied:

- the same card, slider (−60..60 thousandths of an em), "shipped" reset,
  verdict chips and 13/17/22 size switch;
- local storage, a "Copy answers" JSON block, and the `db` collection
  `outliers` when published with the db capability;
- the same split-word zero, so zero is the pair's white as round 389 ships it.

**50 rows.** The first 10 are the rhythm pairs (`rh hy yt th hm`, both
styles), each judged inside "rhythm". The next 40 are ranked. Every row was
checked for three things:

- the word carries the pair at its index (0 mismatches);
- the row's `k` equals the pair's GPOS kern in the embedded fonts (0
  mismatches);
- the script passes `node --check`.

**Key:** `bench/outliers-2026-09-25.key.json` holds every row's evidence. None
of it is on the page. The full measured pool, 446 roman and 440 italic pairs,
is in `bench/outliers-2026-09-25.evidence.json`.

**Ranking.** `score = E × log10(n)`. n is the pair's count in his books
(`pair_census.py`). E combines up to four signed estimates:

- r (weight 2, his eye);
- d4 and d5 (weight 1 each);
- m (weight 1): for an unjudged pair that carries an explicit `kern.PAIRS` kern
  and whose ink moved or was redrawn since 09-20 (raster IoU below 0.97), how
  far its white moved since then. This is the double-count check. `gr` is its
  clearest case: the g was redrawn and the pair's white moved +52.

E is the weighted mean's magnitude, times the weight share agreeing with its
sign, halved when only one reading exists.

**Excluded from the ranking:**

- pairs set in rounds 388 and 389;
- the roman's ligated `fi fl ff`;
- capital+capital pairs;
- pairs under the count floors: 400 for lowercase, 120 for capital+lowercase,
  400 for marks.

log10 is chosen over raw n so that `th` (40,742) cannot bury a pair 20 units
off.

**The corpus shrank.** `pair_census.py` now finds **22 books** (1,598,289 pair
instances) under `~/src/claude-tools`, where it found 36 (2,007,794) on 09-20.
The counts here are from the 22.

**Turning his answers into kerns** (explicit pairs, not a refit):

```bash
cd tools/wedge_serif
source ./build_env.sh && albo_build_all DIR
python3 bench_outliers.py kerns answers.json --built DIR   # or an ArtifactData out_dir of `outliers`
```

For each answered pair, the target is the white in the page's fonts plus his
delta. The script adds the target minus the white in DIR, and prints a kern.py
block in round 388/389's form. The arithmetic is relative to the page's fonts,
so a glyph that moves between the page and the kern round is not paid twice. A
0 answer means "shipped is right", and the script writes no kern for it.
Tested on a synthetic set: `gr` −30 → add −30, `yt` +15 → add +15, `r,` 0 →
no kern.
