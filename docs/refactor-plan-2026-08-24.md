# Refactor and simplification plan — 2026-08-24

Owner asked for "best plan to refactor and simplify codebase." This is that
plan, and every size in it is measured rather than estimated. It covers BOTH
repos; it lives here because the simulator is where the surface mass is.

**The organizing principle is not tidiness.** The stated goal is that future
agentic work be cheaper and nothing be overbuilt. The cost of agentic work is
not total line count — it is (a) how much context an agent must load to make one
change, and (b) how often two agents must be serialized because they need the
same file. Both were paid in real time on 2026-08-24: three separate tasks
queued behind `src/HalDisplay.cpp`, and one deploy was fired against a tree
another agent was still editing. So the plan is ordered by **contention and
context cost**, not by what looks untidy.

## Tier 1 — delete generated artifacts for a retired configuration

**The single largest and safest item, by two orders of magnitude.**

`lib/EpdFont/builtinFonts/*_3x.h` — **62 files, 495,933 lines, 44 MB.** Each
carries `generated by fontconvert.py`. They are included only under
`#if CROSSPOINT_RENDER_SCALE >= 3` (`src/main.cpp:93`).

The 3x tier was dropped on 2026-08-23 ("drop 3x support for now"). iOS is frozen
at 2 and the desktop defaults to 1, so **no shipped or buildable configuration
compiles these files.** They are half a million lines of generated output for a
tier nothing selects.

**CORRECTION, same day.** The paragraph above originally said these files are
compiled by nothing. That is true of every SHIPPED configuration and false in
general: `CROSSPOINT_RENDER_SCALE=3` remains a supported desktop opt-in, so
`#if CROSSPOINT_RENDER_SCALE >= 3` is a condition a developer can still select.
They are conditionally live, not dead.

The correction matters because of what prompted it — the owner asked whether 3x
would work at native resolution on an iPad Pro or a MacBook Pro, and the
arithmetic says **yes on both, and on every Mac**: 3x makes the framebuffer
1584x2376, which fits at 1.02 on an iPad Pro 11", 1.16 on a 13", and 1.24-1.41
on the MacBook Pros, against 0.80 on an iPhone Air. The full table is in
`docs/ios-render-scale.md`. So the tier is retired for the phone on evidence
that does not apply to the larger displays, and these files are the assets a
tablet or Mac build would need.

They are still kept because re-enabling 3x is documented as "one number in
`ios/CMakeLists.txt`", and that promise survives deletion because these are
*generated*: the recipe is `fontconvert.py` and it still works. But the honest
framing of Tier 1 is **"delete regenerable artifacts and document the
regeneration"**, not "delete dead code" — a weaker claim, and one that turns a
working env var into a regenerate-first step. Decide it on that basis.

**Gate before deleting anything: prove reproducibility.** Regenerate two or
three of them and diff byte-for-byte against what is committed. If the generator
does not reproduce them exactly, they are not artifacts, they are source — stop,
and report that instead. This check is the whole risk of Tier 1.

Expected: firmware repo drops ~44 MB and ~496k lines; every `grep` and every
agent file-scan across `lib/` gets proportionally cheaper.

## Tier 2 — break the contention monolith

`src/HalDisplay.cpp` at 5,068 lines is the file every visual change must load,
and the reason three tasks serialized on 2026-08-24. It holds: the panel
palette and its drift, the 1bpp→ARGB conversion, present coalescing, the sleep
and collapse path, the warm-up, texture caches, and the compositing of every
surface pass.

The models are ALREADY pure headers (`Letterpress.h`, `Scanlines.h`,
`PhosphorGrain.h`, `ShowThrough.h`, `LaidStructure.h`, `CornerDefocus.h`,
`PowerOffCollapse.h`, `PowerOnWarmUp.h`). What sits in `HalDisplay.cpp` is only
their *compositing* — texture build, cache key, blend. That is the part to move.

Proposed split, each a translation unit:

- **`HalDisplay.cpp` (core)** — SDL lifecycle, texture, present policy, palette
  reads, the conversion. Target ≤ 1,500 lines.
- **`SurfaceSheet.cpp`** — the light stack: letterpress, tooth, formation, laid
  structure, show-through, defects, drift. These share the paper darkening
  budget and one cache key, so they belong together.
- **`SurfaceTube.cpp`** — the dark stack: scanlines, corner defocus, grain.
- **`SurfacePower.cpp`** — collapse and warm-up compositing.

**The budget arithmetic must not split.** `FieldSelection.h` already holds the
one decision about which fields composite and their budget shares; it is what
made this split tractable and it stays the single authority.

`ios/CrossPointIOSShim.cpp` (3,005 lines) is the same shape and the second
candidate: pad layout, hit tests, tap synthesis, and the harness hooks are four
separable concerns.

## Tier 3 — resolve the frozen surface

13 getters return constants. 22 dial rows exist. 2,273 lines of picker UI cannot
be opened from the phone.

This is **an owner decision, not an engineering one**, because the ruling was
"take out paper and crt settings *for now*" — explicitly reversible. The options,
with their costs:

1. **Keep and mark** (status quo since 2026-08-24). Zero risk, zero saving. The
   inertness is now documented at each drawer, which was the actual defect —
   three comments claimed the drawers still worked.
2. **Move behind a build flag.** The code stays in git and out of the binary.
   Saves app size, keeps reversibility, costs one flag and a CI path nobody
   exercises — and an unexercised path is how the `-DCROSSPOINT_SIM_HOST_WIFI`
   family earned its escape hatch.
3. **Delete.** Largest saving, and a one-way door against a ruling that says
   "for now."

Recommendation: **1 until the owner says the freeze is permanent, then 3.**
Do not do 2 — it buys little and adds a configuration nobody builds.

## Tier 4 — extend the pattern that is already working

Six pure, host-tested decision headers exist and each replaced a class of silent
bug: `FieldSelection.h`, `PanelSource.h`, `SheetIdentity.h`,
`HostKeyboardState.h`, `TextEntryKeyRouting.h`, `FrozenPage.h`. Every one of
them was extracted because the same decision had been computed in two places and
the two drifted.

As Tier 2 splits `HalDisplay.cpp`, every decision that crosses a new file
boundary should become one of these rather than a shared mutable atomic read
twice. That is the mechanical version of "nothing is overbuilt": the model is
pure, the test is cheap, and the failure mode stops being invisible.

## What NOT to do — negative results, recorded so they are not re-proposed

- **Do not strip or relocate comments.** 38–54% comment density reads as bloat
  and is the opposite: these comments record dated incidents ("shipped once,
  build 123", "cost two investigations") and are why those bugs have not
  recurred. Moving them into `docs/` would defeat the mechanism, which is being
  *at the code* the next agent edits. The token cost is real and it is the price
  of not re-paying for the same bug.
- **Do not delete the pickers** while the ruling says "for now" (Tier 3).
- **Do not touch `src/stb_image.h`** (7,988 lines, vendored third party).
- **Do not consolidate the 14 surface-model headers into fewer files.** They are
  small, pure and individually tested; merging them would rebuild the monolith
  Tier 2 is dismantling.
- **Do not "simplify" the HAL stubs.** Their public surface must mirror the
  firmware's exactly or the link breaks — that is the contract, not redundancy.

## Sequencing, and the gates

1. Tier 1 first: largest, safest, and it makes every later scan cheaper. Gate =
   byte-exact regeneration proof.
2. Tier 2 next, ONE unit at a time, each landing green on `tests/run_all.sh`,
   the desktop canary and an iOS build. Gate = a rendered page byte-identical to
   before the split; a compositing refactor that changes a pixel has a bug in it.
3. Tier 4 continuously, as part of Tier 2 rather than as its own pass.
4. Tier 3 only on an owner ruling.

**Adversarial review after each tier, not at the end.** On 2026-08-24 it found
the day's only memory-safety bug and disproved one of my own fixes; a refactor
of this size without it would ship a silent regression.

**One agent per directory.** Two or three at once, partitioned, and never a
deploy while any of them is live — both rules were broken on 2026-08-24 and both
cost time the same day.

## What this is worth

Tier 1 alone removes roughly half a million lines from the firmware tree. Tier 2
turns the one file that serializes visual work into four that do not. Neither
changes a pixel, and both are verifiable by measurement rather than by opinion —
which is the only kind of refactor worth running against a codebase whose whole
value is what it renders.
