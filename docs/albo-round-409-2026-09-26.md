## 3. Spacing: refit on his ABSOLUTE whites

**The problem.** Every italic reading is a delta `d` on the 09-20 bench zero,
so it names an absolute white: `T = white0920 + d`. Re-drawing the outline
moves the white the fit rule gives a pair before any table touches it. Refitting
the old deltas unchanged would make the model reproduce the old deltas, not his
whites. Unrefit arm m measured this: the letters' own white grew from 200.9 to
221.5 units per character (+10%).

**What was done** (`local_ai/b2_fit.py`, round 409 block):

1. **The outline's own effect on every pair.**
   `delta(p) = white(new outline, same tables) - white(old outline, same tables)`.
   - The two builds are identical in every table, kern, hold, clearance and
     tracking, so all of those cancel. They are kept in
     `bench/italic-delta-r409/` (`-old` is round 408's outline, `-new` is round
     409's, both on round 405's tables).
   - "White" for `delta` is the **gap between the two glyphs' ink extremes
     inside the x band**, at 1 unit per pixel. That is the band the fit rule
     spaces on.
   - The bbox white is used only where a glyph has no x-band ink (the quotes).
   - **Why not bbox everywhere:** a stretched ascender or tail reads as
     spacing. `d-e`, `d-o` and `d-comma` read +19 to +21 on the bbox and 0 in
     the band. Over 188 bench pairs the medians are +2 (bbox) and +4 (band).
2. **Readings are re-based:** `d_new = d - delta(p)`, which is
   `T - (white0920 + delta)`. Every italic reading is shifted: the bench, the
   outlier bench and sessions 1–6.
3. **Features are re-measured on the new glyphs** (`ZeroFont`): each pair is
   placed at its zero white `white0920 + delta`. The `kern` feature keeps the
   09-20 font's own kern, which is what it always meant.
4. **Kept on:** this is now the default in `b2_fit.py`, so later refits and
   benches stay on the new zero. `ALBO_B2_ITALIC_NEW=off` gives the pre-409
   path, which reproduces round 405's tables exactly for both styles (checked).
5. **Ingest needs no change.** A page served on the round-409 italic converts
   to `T - white0920` on the new glyphs, and the new zero is `white0920 +
   delta` there, so `d0920 - delta` is right for those rows too. Session 8's
   rows go through the same shift.

- **The roman is unchanged to the hundredth**, and its tables are
  byte-identical.
- **The italic improves from 9.37 to 9.13** held out. The new-outline
  features describe his whites at least as well as the old ones did.
- **Result:** B2 refit on 720 readings. Italic in-sample 6.86 → 6.80; 25
  letters, 5 marks, 441 kerns (was 432).
- **Common italic pair whites are held.** The x-band gap moves by a
  frequency-weighted **+0.3** units over 1,215 letter pairs (median 0). For
  example: `th` 39 → 39, `he` 97 → 95, `in` 98 → 98, `an` 91 → 93,
  `re` 45 → 41, `at` 98 → 104. The letters got wider and the white did not.

**Holds:** 35 italic pairs, measured by `b2_fit.measure_holds` against the
round-408 build, so each keeps its round-408 bbox white.

- The q pairs move most: `q'` +59, `q’` +63, `q"` +63, `qu` +52 (these were
  −3, +10, +10 and +5).
- **Why:** SETW stretches the q's tail about the origin, and its tip travels
  about 60 units right. Holding the bbox white keeps the tip's distance to the
  next glyph, which is what he set by hand.
- **Flagged:** `qu` is one of the repeats in session 8, so his eye can confirm
  or overturn it.

**Clearance** (`clearance.py`, measured on the built cuts, one pass). The
Italic and BoldItalic blocks were re-derived from empty.

- Italic: `q j` +101, `q f` +88, `q p` +59, `q y` +46, `four j` +10,
  `J j` +8, `four f` +4.
- BoldItalic: `q j` +44, `q f` +35, `q y` +23, `four j` +17, `q p` +15,
  `q n` +5, `four f` +5, `q J` +4.
- All are descender collisions: the longer q tail and j hook meeting.
- The second pass added nothing. **`cmp_touch` is 0/0 on all four cuts.**
- Round 408's BoldItalic `q two` (+8) is no longer needed and is gone.

**What is approximate:**

- `features.py`'s band constants (x-height 429, ascender 770, descender −280)
  were not moved to the italic's new 435-unit x-height. It is a 6-unit shift
  in the band edges for the italic features only.
- His old italic answers were judged on the old glyph shapes. Holding the same
  x-band gap on a wider letter is the translation-exact part. Whether the
  wider letter wants the same gap is his to see, and that is why session 8 is
  italic-heavy.
- The hold compensation uses the bbox white, unchanged from rounds 396–405.
