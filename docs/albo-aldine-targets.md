**2026-09-16**, against commit `e16c6dc` ("albo: bank Flanker Griffo Italic and
Bold Italic as the Aldine comparators"). References: `refs/flanker-griffo-italic.otf`

A **reference to draw against**, not an argument. Every number is the output of

only the prose is hand-written. Where the runs do not say what a letter is,

## Method, in four steps

1. **Flatten** the glyph's outline with fontTools (cubics and quadratics both,
   implied on-curve points included, components decomposed).
2. **Scale** so the reference's own x-height -- the bbox top of its `x` -- lands
   on Albo's **429**, making every number comparable with `pen.DESIGN`: stem
   **S = 84**, cap 674, ascender 770, descender 280.
3. **Unshear** about the baseline by `post.italicAngle` (`x' = x - y*tan`),
   because Albo is drawn upright and sheared at build time. A reference
   measured in its own sheared space describes a letter nobody here can draw.
4. **Cut** with horizontal and vertical lines and report the ink **runs** --
   the one measurement that means the same thing on an outline and on a scan
   of a printed page, and the one a designer can act on.

## How to read a block

`rows` are horizontal cuts, labelled by height as a fraction of the x-height
(of the CAP height for capitals): `.50` is half the x-height, `1.15` is 15%
above it, `-0.30` below the baseline. `cols` are vertical cuts at
`x = lsb + fraction * w`, their runs given in y. A run prints as
`start-end(width)`; positions and widths are both in design units. `scan` is
the printed page where a crop exists, and **where there is a scan, the scan is
the target** -- the outline is a stand-in for the rest.

**Stroke thickness is measured twice and reported only when the two agree.** A
horizontal run across a diagonal or a curve is wider than the stroke is, so the
perpendicular width comes from both cuts (`t = w*h/sqrt(w^2+h^2)`) and is then
checked against the largest disc that fits at the run's midpoint, which a
junction cannot inflate. Samples disagreeing by over 10%, or not locally
parallel over +/-15 units, are dropped; `n=` is how many survived.

**Quote the MEDIAN, not the extremes.** `thickest` and `thinnest` name a
*place* on the letter: on every arched letter (`h m n r u`) the thickest lands
where the arch springs from the stem, which is a real feature and not the stem.

## Two things to know before using a number

**The scale is built on the DRAWN top of `x`, not the declared x-height.** In
both references the whole lowercase -- `x z o n v` -- shares one top and one
bottom, so there is no separate flat x-line in the outline to find. Flanker
declares `OS/2.sxHeight` 900 while drawing that top at 920 (2048 upm), so
**every number here is 2.2% smaller than a declared-x-height scale would
give**; multiply by 1.0222 to convert. On that basis Flanker's lowercase
overshoots ~+10 above the x-line and ~-10 below the baseline, against Albo's
ruling of 14.

**Unshearing Pagella leaves a lean; unshearing Flanker does not.** Fitted to
the `l` stem, Flanker draws 11.8 degrees against a declared 12.0 -- upright to
0.2 degrees. Pagella draws 11.7 and declares 10.0, so **Pagella's unsheared
letters still lean 1.7 degrees** and its bbox widths are inflated by roughly
`height * 0.03`. Use it for stroke widths and proportion, not for x positions.

## 6. Negative results -- what NOT to draw against

Recorded so the next pass does not re-measure them and reach the same place.

**Scans that fail their own self-check.** The crop's declared x-height is what
every unit is scaled by, so a crop whose ink is not the height that letter
should be is scaled wrong and everything under it with it:

| scan | ink height | expected | what it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| `a` | 1.55 xh | 0.95-1.15 | **do not use.** 26 components before despeckle; the crop is a 160x181 px photograph holding more than the letter. Its band medians (66 / 106) are not one letter's two strokes |
| `q` | 1.13 xh | 1.45-1.85 | the crop cuts the descender. The bowl (band 69 / 70) is usable; nothing below the baseline is in the crop at all |
| `l` | 1.39 xh | 1.45-1.85 | short. And its right-run median of 23 is a neighbour's ink -- an `l` has one stroke |
| `r` | 0.90 xh | 0.95-1.15 | 10% short: either the crop clips or the declared x-height is high |
| `s` | 0.94 xh | 0.95-1.15 | borderline, same two possibilities |
| `u` | 1.17 xh | 0.95-1.15 | over. Its `.50` row shows four runs where a `u` has two, so the crop has caught an adjacent letter |

**`m`'s scan rows are unusable outside the band**: `.50` gives five runs and
`.75` seven, where an `m` has three stems -- at 66 px of x-height the printed
arches break up under Otsu. The band medians (70 / 60) survive; the row detail
does not.

**The shear cannot be removed from any scan, and this is not a bug to fix
later.** The printed angle is declared nowhere, varies letter to letter in a
chancery hand, and at 54-75 px of x-height a fit off one stem is worth about
+/-2 degrees. What survives is exact rather than approximate: a horizontal
shear leaves every horizontal distance at a fixed y alone, so **scan run
WIDTHS are valid as they stand**, while positions and bbox widths are not.

**Twelve lowercase letters have no scan at all** -- `c f g j k n t v w x y z`
-- so their numbers are Flanker's outline, a digital revival and not the
printed page. Nor has any capital.

**Where `thickest` is not a stroke.** On `h m n r u` it lands where the arch
springs from the stem (`r` reports 91 at .47; the r's stem is 70). On `b d l`
it can land on the ascender's 116-wide serif, on `f j` on the descender's
terminal. All are real features of the letter and none is its stroke -- quote
`med`.

**Where `med` is not the stem.** `x y z` have no vertical stem, so `vert med`
(23, 30, 25) falls on the thin stroke; read `rng`. Pagella's `s v w z` (43, 47,
54, 29) go the same way and are not comparable with its other letters' 61.
`i` and `j`'s `horz med` is the **dot** (n=2 and n=3).

**`s` and `c` yield no two-stroke reading**, by construction and not by
failure: both give one run at every height from .25 to .75, so the flank/stem
pair `aldine_autofit` fits on `a b d g h k m n p q r t u` has no meaning on
them -- the same exclusion its `HAS_STEM` already makes.

**Not measured here:** serif shape (only its width, where a cut crosses one),
joins, entry and exit angles, curvature, and anything about the **word image**
-- which is the acceptance test Albo is actually judged on. A letter that
matches every number on this page can still fail it.
