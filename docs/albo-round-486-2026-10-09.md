## Findings, including the negative ones

- **A dark letter is not fixed by narrowing it** (O2 +11.4%): the in-word
  measure is ink over the letter's SLOT. Round 484's narrower u worked because
  the u was pale. Width goes the other way for a dark letter, and Albo's o is
  already the widest of the seven, so no width arm helps the o.
- **The f's paleness is its advance.** Its ink matches the references'
  (0.53 vs 0.54 of the n); its slot is 25% wider (359 vs 288), from
  `BEARING_ADJ['f'] = (5, 25)` and a bar that ends 61 units inside the
  advance. Lengthening the bar (F1) made it PALER (-18.5%): the extra ink
  lands in the next letter's slot. The fix is the bearings (F4), which is the
  bench's table; F4 overrides its one row.
- **In absolute units Albo's o thins are the references'** (37 against
  30-38) and its sides lighter (77 against 80-91); the o is heavy only
  relative to Albo's own lighter stem (64 against 73-85). "The a and o are
  dark" and "the straight letters are pale" are one fact; this round lightens
  the rounds, the other reading (a heavier stem) is a family change and was
  not drawn.
- The a's hood measure at the 55% column did not move with `A_HOOD_W` (the
  column crosses the hood's run, not its arc); the band and the in-word
  numbers did.
- Z and Z1 differ only in the o (contrast respread vs plain weight); Z1
  keeps the ruled 0.5 S hairline floor, Z lowers it to 0.35 S on the o alone.
