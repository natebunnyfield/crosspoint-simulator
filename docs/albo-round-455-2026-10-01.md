## What the ingest had to learn: accented pairs

This was the first bench to ask an accented pair, and two things in the chain could not hold one:

- **The tables conversion returned 0 for a glyph B2 does not know.** B2's tables hold plain letters and five marks; `table_white('í?')` read í's bearings and the ? as zeros and converted the italic í? answer to +11 (bbox: −35). `active_ingest.table_white` now refuses any pair with a non-ASCII letter, which falls back to the bbox conversion.
- **B2 fits plain letters; the build gives an accented letter its BASE letter's kerning** (`kern.py` `_extend_composites`) plus the mark clearances. So an accented answer is fitted as a reading of its base pair when the mark is FAR from the neighbour: its 2-D nearest approach at the zero at least `FAR` = 2.0 × its own gap, and the two pairs kerned alike there (`active_ingest.far_mark`). Then the white he judged is the letters'. Otherwise the mark is what he judged, and the row is recorded with `fit_pair` None and fitted by nothing (`b2_fit.readings` skips it).
  - **uí**: the acute 3.5× its gap from the u (roman 3.2×) → fitted as **ui**, both styles.
  - **í?**: the acute 0.82× its gap from the ? (italic; roman 1.75×) → **not fitted**. That white is set by round 453's 2-D mark clearance (no mark nearer a neighbour than 0.8 of its own gap); his italic +9 asks for 0.95. How it should move is the owner's ruling (asked), not the fit's.
