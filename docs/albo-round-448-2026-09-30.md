# Albo round 448 (exploration): the italic r ends the way the c does (2026-09-30)

Owner, on round 447's G1–G3 (the pen-stroke drops): *"nope, try again"*. Before that, on round 446: *"446 is terrible. please do this well, no shitty terminal"*.

## The finding: every reference r repeats its own c's top

Laid c beside r, one x-height, per face (`instruments/cr_grid.py`, new this round):

| Face | Top of the c | End of the r |
|---|---|---|
| Coelacanth | a pen terminal: arch over, a cut at the lower left, concave underside | the same terminal |
| Pagella | a short vertical drop, cut flat | the same drop |
| Poetica | a lobe with a notch | the same lobe |
| Flanker | a ball | a ball |
| **Albo 446** | the drawn wedge of rounds 434/436 (approved): arch over, a straight face at 238°, a concave cubic underside | **a different shape:** a sideways flag, a long straight face at 232°, a straight underside with a V-notch |

**Albo was the only face where the two disagree.** Rounds 441–447 tuned the r's end as a letter on its own, against the r's of other faces. That was the wrong comparison: the right one is the r against its own c. Checked against source; the reference readings are by eye at 170–220 px x-height, and all four are unambiguous.

This is also what "pen edge and metal punch like rest" (2026-09-29) asks for, read literally. The rest of Albo's italic finials are drawn wedges with one straight cut. The c's top is the one the r corresponds to, stroke for stroke.

## The construction: the c's own dials on the drawn terminal

`_r_drawn_term` (`R_TERM` 1) already has the c-top's structure: an outer cubic to a tip T, a straight face T → Lw, and an underside Lw → I. What was not the c's:
- **The face:** 0.30 xh at 232° against the c's 0.14 xh at 238°.
- **The underside:** STRAIGHT (`R_TUB` 0) against the c's concave cubic leaving Lw at 120°. The straight underside is what cut the V-notch.
- **The tip:** high at the crest (0.95 xh) against the c's hang.

## Checks

- **Spacing:** each candidate was built with its own spacing table, and the white after the r was held at round 441's by `instruments/r_hold_white.py` (two passes). The final report is empty in both styles for all three: every pair is within 2.5 units.
- **Hairlines:** `cmp_contour_hairs.py --letters` passes on all six cuts.
- **Turns in the outline:** the terminal has no new sharp turn. The notch apex is a round of 13–30° steps. The one reversal in the Bold Italic r (158.6° at the crotch, (255,287)) is present in 446 unchanged and is not in the terminal.

## Negative results (this round)

- **N1–N4, the snipped arm again** (`R_TERM` 0 with `R_SCAP` 0 and a filed cut, 2026-09-30 morning; not published):
  - N1, a nib-angle cut: a spur at the top right with a hook under it;
  - N2, 441's plumb cut, thicker: a blunt block;
  - N3, a slight hang: a claw curling back under the end;
  - N4, an edged-pen drop: a bird's-head notch.
  Every snipped-arm end with a diagonal cut grew a hook or a notch. This confirms round 447's result.
- **Two dials that do not exist.** `ALBO_ALD_R_TX` and `ALBO_ALD_R_TY` are NOT dials. `_rt(k, v)` reads `"ALBO_ALD_R_T" + k`, so the tip is `ALBO_ALD_R_TTX` / `ALBO_ALD_R_TTY`. Five variants were laddered on the wrong names with the tip unmoved, and only the `[r-term]` debug line showed it. `instruments/r_variants.py` now prints that line for every variant. Read it before reading the picture.
- **A filed corner on a cubic underside.** `R_TCH` with `R_TUB` leaves a sliver (C8). The chamfer is a triangle along the underside's first chord, and a curling underside leaves it behind. The c has no filed corner, so the candidates carry none.
- **The c's face length copied literally** (`TF` 0.14, C2/C3): the r's end reads as a thin horn. The r's arm feeds the terminal from a hairline where the c feeds it from a heavy crown, so the face is longer on the r to carry the same weight.
