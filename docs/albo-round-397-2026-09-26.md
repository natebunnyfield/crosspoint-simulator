## 3. The j family, and bearing against kerns

**Does B2 handle the j?** Yes, but mostly through **kerns**, not a bearing:

| | j bearing (lsb, rsb), r396 → r397 | its j kerns in r397 |
|---|---|---|
| roman | (0, +2) → (+8, +2) | `oj` +32 `bj` +29 `ej` +28 `ij` +28 `nj` +25 `aj` +24, `j.` −15 |
| italic | none → (0, +9) | `ij` +21 `ja` +21 `jo` +21 `je` +20 `oj` +17 `aj` +15 `bj` +14 `ju` +14 |

**Why.** A glyph's own preference is split between its identity term and the
shape features, because the features describe the glyph's own profile too. The
ridge penalizes identity, so the shared +24 to +32 on every roman x+j pair lands
in six kerns rather than in a j left-bearing of about +28.

**Does a per-glyph bearing beat many kerns?**
- On the pairs the model covers, it cannot: the two are the same white by
  construction.
- It differs in two places:
  - **The table.** A bearing replaces the kerns.
  - **The rarer pairs outside the kern scope.** A bearing moves them too, and
    kerns do not.
- **Measured** as `b2_fit.py --consolidate`: move each lowercase or mark side's
  median remainder into its bearing, when it has 4+ pairs and |median| ≥ 4,
  then recompute the kerns.
  - Kerns fall from 424 to **235** (roman) and from 451 to **258** (italic).
  - Every in-scope pair keeps its white to rounding.
  - It moves **26 roman and 38 italic glyph sides**. Examples:
    - roman j lsb +28, italic j rsb +14;
    - roman e lsb +10, roman `'` lsb −27;
    - italic f lsb +15.
  - Every rare pair on those sides shifts with them. That includes the **g
    pairs**, which sit outside the kern scope by ruling, so `ge`, `go` and `gh`
    would take the e's and o's new bearings uncorrected.
- **Not shipped. It is an option for the owner.** It is a real change for rare
  pairs, and it widens the uncorrected-g effect.
