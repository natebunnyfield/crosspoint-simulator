# Albo round 453: the italic acute steeper and shorter; every italic mark on its stems' own axis (2026-09-30)

Owner, on the "aquí?" question page (round 452's review finding F1, https://claude.ai/artifact/7cvsbckU4SJdvsj3gvvYAu): *"reduce the accent length and/or make angle of stroke to avoid near collision"*.

**The problem (F1).** Round 452 centered the italic marks on their stems and set the acute +0.06 of the x-height right, as every reference sets an acute. Over the narrow í the acute's top-right tip then met a following ?, and the composite clearance had to open í? to +47 (Italic) and +37 (Bold Italic), against +7 after a plain i. The phrase occurs 48 times in the owner's Spanish book ("¿Usted vive aquí?", "¿Sos de aquí?"). The page offered K (as shipped in build 277) and C (the acute centered on the í's stem); the owner asked for neither, but for the acute itself to change.

## Measured: the italic acute against the references

On its own letters (í é á ó ú, the accented raster minus its base, each font as set; `tools/wedge_serif/instruments/acute_geom.py`):

|---|---|---|---|
| Albo Italic, build 277 | 0.580 | 57.1° | 0.099 |
| Albo Bold Italic, build 277 | 0.614 | 57.0° | 0.160 |
| Pagella Italic | 0.550 | 52.2° | 0.092 |
| Coelacanth | 0.640 | 40.8° | 0.108 |
| Georgia Italic | 0.555 | 45.1° | 0.122 |
| Times Italic | 0.518 | 35.6° | 0.105 |
| Flanker Griffo Italic | 0.529 | 44.8° | 0.088 |
| Pagella Bold Italic | 0.598 | 52.3° | 0.128 |
| Georgia Bold Italic | 0.612 | 50.6° | 0.159 |

Albo's italic acute was the FLATTEST of all of them (57° against 36–52, median 45) and longer than the median (0.580 against 0.550). A flat acute reaches its tip toward the next letter; that is the near collision.

## The drawing

`outlines/glyphs/accents.py` `g_acute`, the italics only:
- `ACUTE_IT_LEAN` 47° from vertical, AS SET: the drawing box is solved from the shear (k = tan 13°), so the lean is the one the reader sees. Width = height × (tan 47° − k).
- `ACUTE_IT_LEN` 0.95 of round 452's set centerline length.
- `ACUTE_IT_WT` 0.96: the broad pen runs about 4% heavier on the steeper stroke (Italic 0.099 → 0.103, Bold Italic 0.160 → 0.168 of the x-height); this holds round 450's measured weight (now 0.101 / 0.162).
- Result: Italic 0.554 long at 47.0°, Bold Italic 0.590 at 47.4°.

Untouched: the grave, the roman, every other mark. The double acute's strokes were already about 45° as set in the italic. The Greek tonos is the acute's glyph, so it is steeper too (round 452's review F5 had it right of the Greek references).

The acute is taller (centerline height 122 → 147 units), so the italic ĺ now tops out at 1014 against the 1015 cap under the reader's declared ascent (Bold Italic ĺ 1015, held there by the cap since round 451).

## The italic marks, on the stems continued up on their own axis

Owner, on the macron page (https://claude.ai/artifact/3ANF9nKq9aZsHdAnwUwsj8): *"the macron or other marks are likely not centered properly for italic. imagine stems continuing up on their axis and center optically based off of that"*.

**What round 452 did, and why it missed.** It read a letter's stems in ONE thin band at 0.75 of its height and carried that point up the FONT's 13° slope. A letter's stems have their own slope. Measured from the outline over 0.15–0.85 of the height (rows whose run count is the dominant one, so a serif or a join cannot pull a line over): the o's sides lean 7°, the u's stems 16°, the ı 13.1°, and in the Bold Italic the ı leans only 9.3°. So each mark landed where the font's slope pointed, not where its stems do. Against the references, measured on exactly the owner's axis, Albo's marks were off by: ō 0.06 right, ē 0.08 right, ñ 0.09 right, ā 0.06 left, Ř 0.16 right, Ĉ Č 0.17 left (Italic; mean 0.039 of the x-height over 18 letters, Bold Italic 0.051).

**A first instrument lied, and is recorded.** A quick drawing tool fitted each stem through EVERY row of its run, and the rows where a stem's serif splits off or where two stems join pulled its lines over: it read the ı at 7.5° and the u's stems at 5–7°, and I told the owner so before the robust fit (dominant run count only) read 13.1° and 16°. The robust fit is the instrument (`instruments/stem_axis_marks.py`); the drawing tool is gone.

**The first axis, rejected.** The first build fitted a straight line through EACH SIDE of the letter (the leftmost and rightmost ink runs over 0.15–0.85 of its height) and took their average. The owner, on its proof: *"your axis calculation is off for many letters"*. He was right: a line fitted through a ROUND side is the curve's average lean, not the letter's slant (the o's sides read 7°, the e's 0°, the a's bowl 22°), and on the R or the E the "right side" is a bowl, a leg or the arms, not a stem.

**Three more passes, each measured on the references** (`instruments/stem_axis_marks.py`; how tightly the five italic references agree around each axis, as the mean absolute deviation of their per-letter mark offsets from their own median):
- *the whole body's centre, unslanted*: 0.033; centres the a e o, but references then sit 0.03–0.10 left on the lowercase;
- *the TOP band's centre* (where the stems arrive under the mark): 0.034; the L's and h's stem, the A's apex, but the entry serifs of i u n pull it left, and a top band sees only the c's back;
- *the band 0.25–0.55 below the top for stem letters, the whole body for the stemless* (c C s S z Z G E F): **0.030**, the tightest, and the references centre within 0.04 of it on nearly every letter.

**The slope is the font's, for every letter.** A per-letter "straight stem" test (a side whose run centres fit a line within 1.5% of the x-height) sat on its threshold for the Bold Italic's calligraphic stems (the n's sides 7.8° and 2.9°, the a's 18°) and tipped differently in the build and in the instrument, which then disagreed by 0.15 of the x-height on the same letter. Every letter now takes the font's own stem slope: the dotless i's, or the I's for a capital (13.1° Italic, 9.3° Bold Italic). In the Italic that differs from a letter's own straight stems by at most ~0.03 of the x-height, which the optical table absorbs.

**The rule now** (`outlines/build.py` `stem_axis_line`, `ALBO_STEM_AXIS`, the italics only):
- the AXIS: the font's stem slope through the centre of the unslanted ink between 0.25 and 0.55 of the x-height (cap height) below the letter's own top, for a letter with stems; through the centre of its unslanted body (0.15–0.85 above the baseline), for a letter without (c C s S z Z G E F);
- the mark's ink centroid goes on the axis at its own height, PLUS the references' optical offset for that letter (`glyphs/mark_axis_it.py`, regenerated by `instruments/stem_axis_marks.py --write` on exactly this axis): the r −0.054 (toward its stem), the E −0.144 and the R −0.157 (over the stem and the bowl, not the arms or the leg), the C +0.086 and the A +0.066, about 0 elsewhere; the acute, grave and double acute add their own lean, net of the letter's: +0.041 / −0.063 / +0.079 lowercase, +0.057 / −0.097 / +0.107 capitals.

**Result**, every letter's symmetric marks against the references' median on this axis: Italic mean deviation 0.045 → **0.004** (worst 0.186, the E, → 0.016), Bold Italic 0.057 → **0.010** (worst 0.161, the R, → 0.06, the r).

**Negative results kept**: the outer-edge axis (`--edges`) biases e, E, c, z and G by 0.2–0.4 in every reference; the line-per-side axis (`--stems`) is the one the owner rejected.

## The italic marks smaller, and kept clear of their neighbors (2026-10-01)

Owner, on version 3 of the proof (Wörter / Töne / Tête / Të / Těšín / aquí?): *"reduce umlaut and other marks to not be so close to other letters and marks"*.

### Measured first: where the marks crowd, and why no gate saw it

`instruments/mark_crowd.py` (new): the 2-D distance from each accented letter's MARK (the accented raster minus its base's) to the whole ink of its neighbor, shaped by HarfBuzz with each font's own kerning, in x-heights, and as a multiple of the mark's own gap to its own letter. On 50 display pairs and on every accented pair in the owner's books (386 pairs, 17,677 occurrences, mostly Spanish). Distances in x-heights, version 3 ("before") against the italic references:

| pair | before | Pagella | Coelacanth | Georgia | Times | Flanker |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Të | 0.031 | 0.174 | 0.233 | 0.148 | 0.243 | 0.121 |
| Tè | 0.035 | 0.230 | 0.190 | 0.247 | 0.299 | 0.184 |
| Tě | 0.043 | 0.140 | 0.213 | 0.205 | 0.349 | 0.167 |
| Tö | 0.081 | 0.201 | 0.233 | 0.184 | 0.300 | 0.035 |
| Tê | 0.082 | 0.112 | 0.271 | 0.182 | 0.284 | 0.161 |
| Tä | 0.104 | 0.233 | 0.397 | 0.195 | 0.300 | 0.093 |
| Vä | 0.140 | 0.207 | 0.326 | 0.135 | 0.258 | 0.159 |
| Wö | 0.174 | 0.236 | 0.223 | 0.139 | 0.261 | 0.144 |
| í? | 0.052 | 0.197 | 0.090 | 0.259 | 0.268 | 0.090 |

The Bold Italic was worse: Wö 0.033, Vö 0.031, Tö 0.034, Të 0.035, Tä 0.033, Wä 0.033, Vä 0.028 (12 to 15 units), and ëï 0.048 (the four dots nearly one row), against its references' 0.108-0.234 (Pagella, Georgia, Times Bold Italic; Flanker's T again the exception).

1. **The crowding is the capital overhang.** The ë's and ö's left dot sits under the beak at the right end of the italic T's arm, or the W's and V's arm serif. The owner's books barely contain these pairs: over the corpus, version 3 had 0.3% of mark occurrences under 0.10 of the x-height (references 0.0-0.9%), median 0.62 (references 0.49-0.65). Its one close corpus pair was í? (48 occurrences).
2. **Mark to mark in the lowercase was never the problem.** ěš 0.348, ší 0.316, üö 0.490, äö 0.550: inside the references' range (Pagella's ěš is 0.137). The mark-to-mark case is ëï in the Bold Italic, an umlaut against an umlaut, which the 2-D rule below covers.
3. **Why the gates never saw it.** `cmp_touch.py` and `local_ai/clearance.py --composites` measure white ROW BY ROW. A dot tucked under an arm shares no scanline with it, so no contact is read at all; the composite sweep takes a 2-D distance only where the row-wise white is already under 0.05 em, which it never is here. In the Bold Italic the dots' tops do share rows with the arm, so the row-wise check opened those pairs, but only to its 0.015 em floor: hence 12-15 units.
4. **Shrinking alone does not fix it.** The marks drawn smaller with version 3's kerns held: Të 0.031 → 0.066 (10% smaller) → 0.089 (20%), Tö 0.081 → 0.116 → 0.139, still under every reference but Flanker. And regenerating the row-wise clearance on smaller marks would TIGHTEN some Bold Italic pairs, since fewer dots would share the arm's rows. So the spacing has to change too.

### The rule: no mark nearer a neighbor than 0.8 of its gap to its own letter

A mark reads as its own letter's when it is nearer that letter than any other. Measured as (distance to the neighbor) / (the mark's own gap), the references' WORST case over the 13 overhang pairs (Wö Vö Tö Yö Fö Tê Të Tè Tě Tä Wä Vä Fä) is: Pagella 0.83, Coelacanth 0.78, Times 0.92, Georgia 0.47, Flanker 0.16 (its Tö); Bold Italic Pagella 0.63, Times 0.89, Georgia 0.59, Flanker 0. Version 3 had Të 0.15, Tè 0.25, Tö 0.38 in the Italic and 0.14-0.16 across the Bold Italic.

`instruments/mark_crowd.py --clear BUILD --k 0.8` sweeps every pair the composite sweep covers (each accented letter with a mark ABOVE, beside `cmp_touch.composite_neighbors`, factored out of `cmp_touch.py` with its output unchanged) and adds the kern that takes each mark to 0.8 of its own gap + 1 unit into the `clearance_composite` block, the block `clearance.py --composites` writes. The italics' composite clearance was regenerated FROM NOTHING, alternating the row-wise and the 2-D pass with a rebuild between, until both added 0 twice in a row (nine passes).

**K = 1.0 was built first and rejected on measurement.** Stricter than every reference; 2,613 kerns; the Bold Italic Wö went to +19 and Fö to +14, positive kerns, holes. K = 0.8: 1,711 kerns with A's marks.

### The drawing

`outlines/glyphs/accents.py`, the italics only, every ABOVE mark: `ALBO_MARK_IT_K` scales the drawing (width and height, never the stroke weight; the tilde keeps its height, already the references' lowest), `ALBO_DOT_IT_K` the dieresis's and dot accent's dots, `ALBO_DIE_IT_W` the white between the dieresis's dots. All default 1.0 (byte-identical to version 3, checked with `cmp_outlines.py`) until the owner picks:

- **A**: 0.90 / 0.95 / 0.70. As set, 8-10% narrower; the dieresis 0.636 → 0.559 of the x-height (12%).
- **B**: 0.80 / 0.90 / 0.55. As set, 16-20% narrower; the dieresis → 0.510, under Pagella's 0.527.

Measured widths (x-height, Italic): acute 0.445 → A 0.408 / B 0.373 (references 0.362-0.498); grave 0.394 → 0.361 / 0.331 (0.263-0.388); circumflex 0.618 → 0.562 / 0.506 (0.546-0.649); caron 0.627 → 0.576 / 0.524 (0.546-0.649); tilde 0.744 → 0.678 / 0.613 (0.678-0.861); macron 0.709 → 0.641 / 0.573 (0.647-0.713); breve 0.636 → 0.583 / 0.529 (0.624-0.680); ring 0.466 → 0.420 / 0.368 (0.386-0.528); double acute 0.737 → 0.662 / 0.587 (0.612-0.761). A stays in or at the edge of the references' range; B falls under it for seven of the eleven.

### What it costs

- **The reader's kern classes.** `fontconvert_sdcard.py` (firmware) folds the pairs into classes by identical rows and columns, and over 255 either way it DROPS THE STYLE'S KERNING ENTIRELY, with a warning and no error. Classes (left/right) at 8 pt: Italic 86/106 before → A 106/150, B 95/141; Bold Italic 91/126 → 106/160, 97/153 (K = 1.0 with A: 119/169). Under the limit, but the first change in a long while to move it by half; it needs a gate before it ships.
- **Lighter at small sizes on the X3 (1×).** `instruments/parts_check.py` over 30 accented letters × 8-18 pt (180 cells), cells where a mark falls under the 4-dark-pixel floor: Italic 43 before, 46 A, 56 B; Bold Italic 3, 12, 16. At the phone's 2×: none in any.
- **The owner's books.** Three pairs change spacing: í? (48 occurrences), í' (3), Bold Italic Fö (3).
- **aquí?** The ? moves 28 units right of version 3 (12 less than build 277): the acute's tip now keeps 0.8 of its gap from the ?. Round 453's steeper acute is what keeps it at +35 and not +47.
